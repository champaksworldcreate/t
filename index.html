Welcome to Programmer's Picnic. This is the introduction paragraph for PPSpeak V5 Premium. In this demo, the page itself contains the complete HTML, CSS, and JavaScript needed to run the premium narration system.
The floating panel is designed to feel polished and useful for real teaching pages. It has a speaking avatar, a progress bar, mini mode, drag support, and a title narration button. It can be used on course pages, lesson pages, notes pages, and interactive tutorials.
Each paragraph is discovered automatically using ids that begin with the word speak. The script sorts them numerically, so speak0 comes before speak1, and speak1 comes before speak2. This makes the reading order stable and easy to manage.
When the player speaks a paragraph, it highlights the active paragraph on the page and scrolls smoothly to it. At the same time, the premium avatar enters speaking mode. The mouth animates, the glow becomes active, and the wave bars bounce to create a lively teaching experience.
The panel also remembers important preferences. If you change the speaking speed, pitch, or voice, those values are stored in local storage. If you move the panel, its position is also saved. If you collapse it into mini mode, that choice can also be restored.
The WhatsApp button is useful for your real teaching pages. A student can read the lesson, listen to the narration, and then directly contact Champak Roy on WhatsApp using the built-in button. This makes the page feel more connected to your teaching business.
You can reuse this exact structure on your other pages. For example, you can add it to your flex and grid lesson, your Java operators lesson, your Spring Boot lesson, or your OpenStreetMap lesson. The main thing is to give your paragraphs speak ids in the right order.
The next premium step after this version would be a photo-avatar version. In that future version, the default illustrated face could be replaced with your real image, while still keeping the floating panel, animated mouth area, and branded teaching experience.
